Engineering design

FOUNDRY

FOUNDRY is the engineering framework of a one-person company. The company has one person, the operator. Its engineering workforce is AI agents from two vendors, Anthropic (Claude) and MiniMax. Built with Claude.

The company has two business units. FOUNDRY is the engineering business: work comes in, it is built, tested, shipped and probed. TIDE-WAVE is the trading business and today it is FOUNDRY's only customer. The engine itself knows nothing about trading; trading plugs in as sources of work and as the product that ships.

Three rules hold it together. Every piece of work is a bug record, and a bug is one step. Nobody pushes to the main branch; only the test farm merges. Every feature ships with a switch that turns it off in seconds without a deploy.

3 · A check-in through Crucible

Cheapest checks first, one merger at the end

A moved base costs only what changedEach stage result is stored under a digest of the bytes it read, so a rebase re-runs only the stages whose inputs changed.
A merge that would void a run waitsAnother check-in that changes files a running stage reads waits at its merge step, in claim order. It is never rejected for waiting.
A busy box is untouchableThe claim lease and box heartbeat answer "is this box busy". No deploy, restart or reclaim touches a box with a run in flight.

4 · The machine farm

Machines by role

The fleet is a set of small Mac machines, each with a committed manifest of the services it runs. Several machines hold more than one role, so the counts below overlap; the notes say where.

The ten named roles

One decision each, no shared knobs

Each manager is a small service over a pure library with its own command line and tests, and an LLM for the judgment calls. A new responsibility goes to the role whose authority covers it, or it needs a new role.

Project ManagerWhat work to do: the next step for every open bug, and how many agents run at once.
Release ManagerWhat ships: GO or HOLD on accepted work, then the deploy and service restarts.
Test ManagerWhat is adequately tested: confidence per build, suite audits. Never skips a test.
Chief of StaffWhat the operator sees: disposes of routine requests, escalates by exception, keeps a same-day veto window.
Infrastructure ManagerWhat runs: the fleet matches its manifests and stays up. Never merges or deploys.
Risk ManagerWhat size we trade: only through registered sizing switches, inside the operator's bounds.
Public Relations ManagerWhat clients hear: the client communication schedule.
Expense ManagerWhat we spend: AI, data and infrastructure costs shown beside trading earnings on one chart.
Tax ManagerWhat we owe and when: realized gains and quarterly estimates, net of deductible costs.
Compliance and Security ManagerWhat we may do and who holds the keys: terms, licences, key and token expiry. Tracks and reminds only.
